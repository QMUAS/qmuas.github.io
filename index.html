Student-built autonomous aircraft.

QMUAS is Queen Mary University of London's team in the IMechE UAS Challenge. Each year we design, build and fly a fixed-wing drone that runs its mission on autopilot, from take-off to a water payload drop and landing, with our safety pilot ready to take manual control at any time.

A Queen Mary Aerospace Society project, based in the School of Engineering and Materials Science and competing in the Institution of Mechanical Engineers' UAS Challenge.

How a season works

The competition runs across the academic year. Every stage is judged, not just the final flight.

  1. Concept paper

    We pick a configuration and show it can meet the mission rules.

  2. Design specification

    A full design and development report: aerodynamics, structures, payload, safety and cost.

  3. Build and test

    Composite layups, 3D printing, wiring, then ground and bench testing of every system.

  4. Flight readiness review

    Judges check the aircraft is safe and airworthy before we're allowed to fly.

  5. Fly-off

    Autonomous take-off, waypoint mission, water drop and landing, in a 20 m box.

The 2025/26 aircraft

Our first concept didn't perform well enough in analysis, so we went back and redesigned it: a bigger wing, a twin-motor layout and a streamlined carbon fibre fuselage.

It's built to fly the whole competition mission on autopilot, including take-off, the water payload drop and landing, with a safety pilot able to take over at any time.

At the 2026 competition it made it through scrutineering, passing the technical and safety checks needed to progress.

How we built it

From first sketch to the airfield, almost everything on the aircraft was designed and made by the team.

Design

We designed the aircraft in CAD and used computational fluid dynamics to compare options and refine its shape over several iterations, checking every change against the competition rules.

Build

The airframe is made in-house from carbon fibre composites and 3D-printed parts, using materials and training from our sponsors. We wired and integrated all of the electronics ourselves.

Test

Before it flies, we test in stages: electronics on the bench, missions in simulation, then ground tests of every system, so problems show up on the ground instead of in the air.

The 2026/27 aircraft starts now.

We're recruiting for next season. No previous experience is needed, and you don't have to be on an aerospace course: last year's team included electrical engineers and foundation-year students.

How to join

  1. Get an Aerospace Society membership on the QMSU website. QMUAS is a society project, so membership is how you join the team. New members should pick the QMUAS Junior Membership.
  2. Come to workshops and junior team projects. Learn the tools, work on UAV concepts and get to know the team.
  3. Move up to the Senior Team. Junior members can progress to the team that builds and flies the competition aircraft.
